# Domain Logic

The domain group captures the 'core set' of real-world rules relating to the legislation.

For example:

- What is an Act?
- What is an Amending Act?
- What is a Provision?
- What is a Schedule?


## Acts

Two types of Acts exist:

1. **Principal Acts**: sets out a legislative intent and has a title reflecting the subject (e.g. Criminal Procedure Act 2008). Many Principal Acts will amend old legislation too, as it replaces it. 
2. **Amending Acts**: amends one or more existing Principle Acts. They will all have the word "Amendment" in their title.

An Act has passed both houses of Parliament and been given 'Royal Assent.'

Prior to this, it is a **Bill**.


### Structure of an Act

An Act may be divided into the following hierarchy:

1. Chapters
2. Parts
3. Divisions
4. Subdivisions

For example, Chapter 1 - Preliminary, Part 1.4 - Definitions, Division 2 - About Evidence, Subdivision 4 - Witnesses.

## Sections

The body text of an Act contains individual sections (in a Bill they are referred to as 'clauses'). 

Sections are identified by a bolded section numbers and a heading. 

There are no rules about the length of a section.

### Structure of a Section

Sections can be divided in the following hierarchies:

|                       | Sections |                       |
|-----------------------|----------|-----------------------|
| (a) Paragraphs        |          | (1) Subsections       |
| (i) Subparagraphs     |          | (a) Paragraphs        |
| (A) Sub-subparagraphs |          | (i) Subparagraphs     |
|                       |          | (A) Sub-subparagraphs |


## Schedules

Acts often include Schedules which 'hang off' the back end of an Act. They can be used for:

- Amendments of other Acts (set out in the Schedule).
- Treaties of Agreements referred to in an Act.
- Procedural or administrative matters.

Schedules carry the same legal effect as a Section, however.

### Structure of a Schedule

An amending Schedule is numbered, with the various provisions being called 'items'.

Procedural or administrative Schedules have numbered provisions called 'clauses' which can be grouped together by 'Parts' or 'Divisions'. They can also be divided by sub-clauses or paragraphs, in the same way as Sections. 


## The numbering system

- Chapters, Parts, Divisions and Subdivisions—1, 2, 3 etc.
  - Chapter 1—Preliminary (chapters are always numbered and headed in an Act)
  - Part 1.1—Overview (parts are headed, can be numbered as 1.1, 1.2, or 1, 2 etc.)
  - Division 4—Outline (Divisions are headed, numbered as 1, 2, 3, 4, etc.)
  - Subdivisions are not headed.
    - Subdivisions appear as 'Cheats, gambling, etc.' in bold italic font.

### Numbering system for Sections

- Sections: 1, 2, 3 etc.
  - Section headers are bolded.
  - Example: Evidence Act 2008 '4 Courts and proceedings to which Act applies'
- Sub-sections: (1), (2), (3), etc.
  - Normal font, indented once from a section.
- Paragraphs: (a), (b), (c), etc. 
  - Normal font, indented once from a subsection.
  - If no preceding subsection, indented once from a section or preceding text.
- Subparagraphs: (i), (ii), (iii), etc.
  - Normal font, indented once from a paragraph.
  - Roman numerals increasing upwards (vi), (vii), etc.
- Sub-Subparagraphs: (A), (B) (C), etc.
  - Normal font, indented once from a subparagraph.
  - Capital lettering increasing through alphabet, if exceeds alphabet, (AA), (AB), (AC), etc.


### Numbering system for Schedules

- Schedules: 1, 2, 3, etc.
  - Example: Criminal Procedure Act 2009 'Schedule 1—Charges on a charge-sheet or indictment'
  - Header font (larger than normal text), bold, em-dash is typically present but not always.
- Amending Items, Clauses: 1, 2, 3, etc.
  - Appear the same as if they are sections.
  - Some unique numbering can occur, e.g. Criminal Procedure Act 2009 Schedule 2 has '1 Common law' followed by '1.1' and '1.2' etc. 

## Examples and Notes

- Examples are headed by 'Example' in bold, followed by a body of text relating to the provision. 
- They are normally preceded by subsections or similar and exist 'within' a section of an Act.
- Notes function the same way, headed by 'Note' in Bold, preceded by a subsection or similar. 
- Notes can be numbered, e.g. 1, 2, 3 with the numbering appearing to the left of the note body text.

## Definitions

- Typically, towards the beginning of an Act.
- Can be located in a 'Dictionary' at the end of an Act.
- Styled in bold italics.
- Sometimes definition is limited to part of the Act (e.g. at the beginning of a Part or Division).
